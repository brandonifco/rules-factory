# Trial 6: a blind second mapping, compared field by field

Part of [#9](https://github.com/brandonifco/rules-factory/issues/9). Trial 5
([`../injection-trial/`](../injection-trial/)) found that mechanical checks caught 1 of 15
corpus-comprehension errors. #9 decided to add a blind second mapping: a second mapper maps the
same slice without seeing the first map, the two are compared field by field, and each
disagreement is a question to resolve against the corpus. This trial measures that comparison
using trial 5's injections.

**The numbers first.**

| | |
|---|---|
| comprehension-stratum injections (trial 5's `human` stratum, scored) | 15 |
| caught by the comparison (a new flag on the injected entry) | **11 of 15** |
| of those, trial 5 had already caught | 1 (`absence-stated-in-other-words`) |
| flags raised by the comparison on the **clean** reference map | **71** |
| injections where neither the injected entry nor its blind partner is touched by a known leak | 1 (caught 1) |

The 71 flags on the clean map are what a reviewer must resolve before any injected error shows
up. The 11 catches are among those flags, not in place of them. Only one of the 15 injections
is free of every leak I know about, so the 11 of 15 is mostly not a measure of an independent
second reading. See [Leakage](#leakage).

## What was compared

- **Reference map:** `examples/hoyle-backgammon/corpus-map.json` at `ecc53b8`, the factory
  copy and commit trial 5's `run-trial.py` pins. It has 30 entries. It is unchanged at `8181aa3`.
- **Blind map:** [`blind-map.json`](blind-map.json), 41 entries, generated by
  [`build.py`](build.py). Both are copied here unchanged. `build.py` still has the absolute
  scratch path it was written with, so it will not run as-is from this folder. The mapper saw
  the backgammon chapter text (pp. 271-280) and `docs/corpus-map.md` and `docs/method.md`,
  byte-identical to `8181aa3`. It did not see the reference map. I did not observe the mapping
  session, so its blindness rests on its own report.
- **Injections:** every injection in `../injection-trial/injections.py` with
  `stratum="human"` that applies to the factory copy. Each is applied by calling that file's
  own `patch` on a deep copy of the reference. That gives the 15 scored injections plus the
  `absence-in-unused-words` probe, which is reported but not counted, as in trial 5.

## The comparator ([`compare.py`](compare.py))

Nothing in it reads the corpus. All of it works on the two maps.

**Alignment is by quoted text, never by id.**
1. Each `evidence` span is tokenised: lower-case words, with `{273}` page markers and `[65]`
   footnote markers removed.
2. The shared text of two spans is difflib's matching blocks of at least 4 tokens.
3. A reference entry and a blind entry are **linked** when the shared text is at least half the
   shorter span and their cited pages are at most 1 apart.
4. Each reference entry is compared with exactly one **partner**: the linked blind entry with
   the highest `cov_ref × cov_blind + name-word Jaccard`. Here `cov` is the shared tokens as a
   fraction of each span. Names only choose among entries that already quote the same text, and
   never create a link. Without the name term, `die-faces` pairs with the opening-throw advice
   paragraph, because the reference quotes the whole enumeration and the blind map quotes one
   sentence of it.
5. A reference entry with no link, or a blind entry with no link, is an **unaligned** flag.

The alignment it produced on the clean map is in `results.json` (`baseline.alignment`). I
checked it by hand. Every partner is the entry a reader would pair it with, with two
exceptions. `stake-multiplier` pairs with `gammon-stake` and not `backgammon-stake`, because
the blind map splits it in two. `inner-table-handedness` and `board-tables` both pair with
`table-designations`, because the blind map merges them.

- **Unaligned reference entries:** none.
- **Unaligned blind entries (5):** `bearing-off-must-play-forward`, `board-compartments`,
  `board-construction`, `calling-the-throw`, `opening-throw-ranking`.
- **Linked but nobody's partner (9, not flagged):** `blot`, `dice-apparatus`,
  `make-point-advice`, `opening-throw-advice`, `other-men-suspended`, `point-colours`,
  `point-count`, `rubber`, `throw-part-lost`. These are mostly places where the blind map split
  one reference entry into several.

**Fields compared on each (reference entry, partner) pair:**
- `kind`, `scope`, `clarity`: equal or not.
- `ambiguity`: present in both or only one; `fate` when both have one.
- `dependsOn` and `gatedBy`, edge by edge. A blind edge is translated to the reference entries
  its target is partner of. An edge to a blind entry that is nobody's partner cannot be
  compared and is skipped.
- `evidence`, as a proxy only. The comparator flags when one span quotes text the other omits
  (coverage below 0.9 in one direction or both). This says the two mappers quoted different
  amounts of text. **It does not test whether the evidence supports the entry.** It can only
  notice a support problem that shows up as a difference in span.

Not compared: `name`, `note`, `status`, `beyondAdapter`, `absentFrom`, `crossReferences`,
`conflict`, and citations. That list was fixed before any injection ran. Two misses below
depend on it.

**Scoring.** A flag's identity is (entry, field, detail, blind partner). Values are left out,
so a flag already raised on a field is not new just because the value changed. An injection is
**CAUGHT** when the injected map has a flag on an injected entry that the clean-map run did not
have. This is the same control-run rule as trial 5's `score.py`. In all 11 catches the new flag
is on the field the injection changed. For the two injections that add a whole entry, any flag
on that entry counts.

### Leakage

The blind mapper reported that the docs it was given state these reference verdicts:
`point-designations`, `direction-of-travel`, `strategy-advice`, `die-faces`, `doubling-cube`,
`game-value`, `must-play-whole-throw`, `stake-multiplier`, `enter-from-bar`,
`legal-destination`, `full-table-suspension`, `bearing-off-doublets`, `bearing-off-eligible`,
`move-by-pip`, `bearing-off-highest`, `starting-position`, `opening-roll`, and the conflict over
the points open to an entering man. I also searched the two docs for each injected entry's
subject and found two more:
- "either thrice or four times (as may have been agreed)" is quoted in both docs as an
  assertion (`agreed-backgammon-multiple`).
- "Twelve points to a table" is in `method.md` (`board-tables`).

A flag counts as leaked when the blind verdict it disagrees with may have come from the docs
rather than the chapter. That happens when the injected entry is on a leak list (**entry**), or
when its blind partner is (**partner**).

| cut | n | caught |
|---|---:|---:|
| injected entry on the mapper's list | 9 | 7 |
| injected entry not on the mapper's list | 6 | 4 |
| entry or partner on the mapper's list | 12 | 9 |
| not on the mapper's list (entry or partner) | 3 | 2 |
| entry or partner on the mapper's list, or found in the docs | 14 | 10 |
| **no leak found** | **1** | **1** (`depends-spurious`) |

The injections were written to reproduce trial 4's findings. Those findings were written up in
the same docs the blind mapper read, so the overlap is not a coincidence. On this corpus, with
these docs, the independent evidence for the method is one injection.

## Baseline: 71 flags on the clean map

By field: `dependsOn` 27, `evidence` 18, `gatedBy` 12, unaligned blind entry 5, `ambiguity.fate`
3, `kind` 2, `clarity` 2, `ambiguity` presence 2. Full records are in `results.json`
(`baseline.flags`).

I resolved each flag against the chapter text. I am not an independent reader: I had the
reference map, its notes and the decision records open while doing it. Verdicts:
- **R**: the corpus supports the reference; the blind map is wrong.

- **U**: the corpus does not settle it.
- **N**: not a disagreement about the corpus. Either both are defensible choices of span or
  granularity, or it is an artefact of the comparator (a split, a merge, or an edge through an

## Limitations

- **Leakage decides how the result can be read.** 14 of the 15 injections touch a verdict the
  blind mapper could have read in the docs. The one with no leak found was caught. That is n = 1.
  Independence is the whole mechanism, and this run mostly does not have it. A clean
  measurement needs a second mapper given docs with the backgammon examples removed, or a
  corpus the docs never discuss.
- **One blind mapper, one reference, one corpus.** Whether a field disagrees depends on what
  this particular mapper happened to write. A different mapper would give a different 11 and a
  different 71.
- **The cost is large next to the catch.** A reviewer resolves 71 flags on a correct map to
  surface errors that each add one to four flags. By my reading, 35 of the 71 are noise from
  granularity and alignment. Adjudicating them is the doubled cost #9 anticipated, and more.
- **The evidence check is a proxy.** It compares span extents between the two maps and never
  asks whether a span says what its entry claims. It caught `evidence-truncated` because the
  spans differed in length. It missed `evidence-adjacent-sentence` because the blind map's
  span happened to contain the injected sentence.
- **Shared omissions are silent.** In `depends-dropped` the blind map also lacked the edge, so
  the injection removed a flag instead of adding one. Any error the blind mapper also makes is
  invisible to the comparison.
- **The fields compared were fixed in advance and leave gaps.** `status`, `beyondAdapter` and
  `name` are not compared. Comparing `status` would have caught `beyond-adapter-false`; that
  was seen after the run and is not counted. Nothing mechanical compares free-text names.
- **Two catches come from an unrelated disagreement.** Each added-entry injection pairs with
  the blind `legal-destination`, and the flag fires because that entry is ambiguous (a leaked
  verdict), not because the comparator recognised a fabricated or falsely absent rule.
- **Alignment is heuristic.** Name similarity breaks ties among spans that overlap. Splits and
  merges make edge translation lossy, and edges to a blind entry that is nobody's partner are
  skipped. The alignment is printed for checking, and I checked it, but I also wrote it.
- **My adjudication of the baseline is not independent.** I resolved the 71 flags with the
  reference, its notes and its decision records in view. The R/B/U/N counts are one reader's.
- **Injected, not real, errors.** As in trial 5, the injections are a purposive sample shaped
